# Additional purchases and displayed return

Researched 20 October 2026 following the user's question about adding to an older profitable investment.

## Important distinctions

- Current value is today's marked value of a position.
- Cost-based gain on a buy-only position is current value minus purchase costs; percentage gain divides that gain by purchase costs. Fees, sales, transfers, and distributions require additional accounting rules.
- Historical portfolio performance across deposits and withdrawals is a different measure. Wealthfront documents time-weighted return as removing the effect of external cash-flow timing, and money-weighted return as reflecting those cash flows. It does not recommend a simple gain/contributions ratio for judging investment-manager performance.
- Forward projections compare future scenarios against today's current-value baseline; they are not historical realized gains.

Source: [Wealthfront return methodology](https://support.wealthfront.com/hc/en-us/articles/209353386-How-do-you-calculate-the-return-displayed-on-my-dashboard-and-account-pages).

## Illustrative arithmetic, not a performance forecast

An investment cost $100 and is now worth $120: its gain over cost is $20, or 20%. An additional $100 purchase at today's price makes combined cost $200 and combined value $220, before fees or price movement: gain remains $20 while combined gain over cost becomes 10%. The added purchase did not create a loss or change the earlier purchase's gain; it changed the combined cost denominator.

## Proposed product treatment

Show historical gain over cost only when purchase data is known. Additional purchases should be separate records, with date and amounts/quantities sufficient to establish cost and value. Support for those records, sales, fees, and cash-flow-adjusted performance is still a product decision. Do not derive historical profit or time-weighted returns from a current-value-only entry or relabel projected percentages as actual profit.
